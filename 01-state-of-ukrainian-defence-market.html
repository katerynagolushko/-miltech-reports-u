Ecosystem Transformation

The Ukrainian defense market has fundamentally restructured around what Olena Dushyna, CEO of L7 Defense Group, describes as four pillars: Business (tech creator), Defense (field validator), State (regulator/coordinator), and Market (competitive selector). This horizontal network replaced the pre-war vertical hierarchy where the state was the sole customer and regulator.

The transformation is visible across every category. The simulator market grew from 2-3 companies pre-war to around 60 companies, with 5-6 serious players now competing, according to Dushyna. Similarly, 280+ companies now work on UGVs (uncrewed ground vehicles), having completed over 22,000 robotic missions, with 50,000 robotic systems planned for procurement this year, reports Ross Chayka.

This shift from monopoly to competition created a self-reinforcing cycle. Companies iterate based on direct feedback from military units in the field. Products that fail in combat are eliminated within weeks. Winners scale rapidly through multiple procurement channels, including direct contracts with brigades, the Brave1 marketplace, and volunteer funds.

Market Size & Funding

Defense Budget & Platform Growth

Brave1 platform budget scaled 15x in 3 years; overall defense spending approaching $100B

The Brave1 platform emerged as the primary funding and certification hub for defense startups. As of 2026, Brave1 has registered approximately 3,500 projects, issued 260 grants, and distributed 1.3 billion UAH, according to Olena Dushyna citing official government data. The platform's budget grew 15x from 100 million UAH in 2023 to 1.5 billion UAH today.

This ecosystem growth sits within a broader defense spending surge. Ukraine's defense budget is approaching $100 billion, with the majority funded through EU-backed credit, reports Ross Chayka. The scale of this market has catalyzed defense tech entrepreneurship across Europe, with 230+ European defense startups founded after February 2022.

The rapid capital deployment reflects both urgency and pragmatism. Traditional defense procurement timelines of 5-7 years compressed to 6-month cycles. Funding flows through multiple parallel channels including Brave1 grants, volunteer funds (Come Back Alive, Prytula Foundation), international government contracts (Danish Model), and direct brigade procurement.

Innovation & Technology Leadership

Ukraine's innovation cycle runs at approximately 6 months per iteration, compared to Europe's 5-7 years for STANAG certification and fielding, according to Ross Chayka. This 10-12x speed advantage stems from necessity and direct military feedback loops. Companies develop prototypes, test them in combat within weeks, iterate based on battlefield data, and scale production within months.

The production scale is unmatched. Ukraine produced 5-7 million drones in 2025 and is targeting 10 million in 2026. No other European or Allied facility reaches this volume. Chayka describes Ukraine as "the largest defense tech polygon in Europe and the world", emphasizing that the combination of production capacity and live combat testing creates advantages that cannot be replicated in peacetime environments.

This velocity applies across categories. FPV drones evolved from garage projects to commodity products in under 18 months. The innovation frontier shifted to AI targeting systems, fiber-optic control, mesh networks, and autonomous swarms. Technologies that would require years of lab testing in Western countries prove themselves (or fail) within combat cycles measured in weeks.

Key Takeaways for Defense Tech Founders

1. Unmatched production scale and field-testing velocity

5-10 million drones produced annually, 280+ UGV companies, 22,000 robotic missions completed, 50,000 robots planned for 2026 procurement. No other country operates at this scale. Ukraine serves as the world's largest live testing ground for defense technology.

2. Innovation cycles 10-12x faster than Europe

6-month iteration cycles vs. 5-7 years for European STANAG certification and fielding. Products prove themselves in combat within weeks, not lab environments over years. FPV drones went from garage projects to commodity products in under 18 months.

3. Market transformed from state monopolies to competitive ecosystems

Simulators: 2-3 companies (pre-war) to 60+ companies (5-6 serious players like L7 Defense). UGVs: 5 companies to 280+ companies. Drones: 10 companies to 500+ companies. New four-pillar paradigm: Business, Defense, State, Market working horizontally instead of vertically.

4. $100B defense budget with multiple funding channels

Brave1 platform: 3,500 registered projects, 260 grants, 1.3B UAH distributed (budget grew 15x from 100M to 1.5B UAH). Overall defense spending approaching $100B (majority EU-backed credit). Parallel channels include volunteer funds, international government contracts (Danish Model), and direct brigade procurement.

5. Major certification barrier: 16-month codification process

L7 Defense took 16 months to codify their first simulator product batch in late 2023. Simplified procedures exist for drones and EW systems, but most categories still require extensive state certification before entering procurement. This creates a moat for companies that successfully navigate the process.
