VAT Exemption for Defense Production

Dmytro Mikhailenko (Crowe Mikhailenko) outlines the VAT framework for defense tech companies. Tax Code of Ukraine article 197.11 exempts from VAT: (1) supply of goods/services within Ukraine's customs territory and import of goods as international technical aid under Ukraine's international treaties, (2) supply of goods/services and import financed by international aid under international treaties, (3) import of property as humanitarian aid under the Law on Humanitarian Aid.

The practical implication: defense companies selling to Ukrainian government or receiving international technical aid don't collect VAT, improving cash flow and pricing competitiveness. The procedure for attracting, using, and monitoring international technical aid is governed by Cabinet of Ministers resolution dated 15.02.20xx. Companies must register with relevant authorities and maintain documentation proving aid qualifies under international treaty provisions.

For companies with both defense and civilian revenue streams, VAT accounting separates: defense sales are VAT-exempt, civilian sales carry standard VAT. This creates administrative overhead but significant savings on defense portion. Companies should structure contracts to maximize defense classification where possible, though misclassification risks penalties.

Defence City vs. Diia City: Tax Regime Comparison

Defence City resident status provides tax breaks specifically for defense production companies. Benefits include simplified taxation, reduced corporate profit tax rate, and other incentives designed to stimulate domestic defense manufacturing. The qualification criteria focus on defense-related output: companies must demonstrate that majority revenue comes from defense products or services.

Diia City is Ukraine's tech special economic zone offering favorable tax treatment for IT companies: 5% personal income tax for employees, simplified corporate taxation, no military tax for employees. Originally designed for civilian IT, many defense tech companies qualify because their products are software-intensive (targeting systems, C2 platforms, AI models).

Key insight from Mikhailenko: companies can potentially combine Defence City and Diia City status, layering tax benefits. The legal framework allows dual participation if the company meets both sets of criteria. Practical application: a defense AI company could claim Defence City status for VAT exemption on defense sales while maintaining Diia City status for employee tax benefits. This requires careful legal structuring and likely separate legal entities or divisions, but the combined savings can be substantial.

Mobilization and Employee Reservation 2026

Andriy Dukhnytskyi addresses keeping critical-enterprise status in 2026. Critical-enterprise designation provides employee reservation (бронювання) from mobilization for staff in essential roles. This is the single most important retention tool for defense tech companies because it directly addresses engineers' primary concern: being mobilized mid-project.

Qualification criteria for critical-enterprise status (2026 rules): (1) defense production output meeting specified thresholds, (2) employee count and skill composition requirements, (3) financial stability indicators, (4) formal approval from relevant ministry (typically MoD or Ministry of Strategic Industries). The process involves extensive documentation proving the company's defense contribution and the criticality of specific employee roles.

Reservation is not blanket for all employees. Companies must justify each reserved position with specific duties tied to defense production. Generic roles (admin, sales, marketing) rarely qualify. Technical roles (engineers, DevOps, systems architects, PMs on defense projects) typically qualify if properly documented. The approval authority reviews quarterly and can revoke reservation if production output drops or employee duties change.

Strategic implication: companies should apply for critical-enterprise status before needing it. The approval process takes 2-4 months minimum. Waiting until engineers receive mobilization notices is too late. Best practice: file during scale-up phase (10-15 employees) when the company has enough output to prove criticality but before the team is large enough that mobilization becomes statistically probable.

MoD Civilian Hiring with Reservation

Oleksa Boyko's slide deck notes that MoD is hiring Senior/Middle PMs, Product Owners, Middle-Senior BAs, DevOps, and SysAdmins as civilian posts with reservation (бронювання). This creates both competitive threat and validation: MoD recognizes civilian digital talent is critical and offers reservation to attract it. Private companies competing for the same talent pool must either (1) offer better impact/growth opportunities to offset lack of reservation, or (2) pursue critical-enterprise status to match the reservation benefit.

The government reservation is more secure than private company reservation because it's not tied to quarterly output reviews or ministry approval risk. However, government roles come with bureaucratic constraints that limit impact velocity. For mission-driven engineers who value seeing their code in combat quickly, startup roles may still be more attractive despite less secure reservation status.

Key Takeaways for Defense Tech Founders

1. VAT exemption on defense production (Tax Code 197.11)

Exempts: (1) goods/services + imports as international technical aid, (2) supply + imports financed by international aid, (3) humanitarian aid imports. Defense sales to Ukrainian govt or via international aid = no VAT. Improves cash flow, pricing competitiveness. Mixed revenue: separate accounting (defense exempt, civilian standard VAT).

2. Can combine Defence City + Diia City status for layered tax benefits

Defence City: tax breaks for defense production, simplified taxation, reduced corporate profit tax. Diia City: 5% personal income tax for employees, simplified corporate tax, no military tax. Dual participation possible with proper legal structuring (likely separate entities/divisions). Example: defense AI company uses Defence City for VAT exemption + Diia City for employee tax benefits.

3. Critical-enterprise status provides employee reservation (most important retention tool)

Qualification: (1) defense output thresholds, (2) employee count + skill mix, (3) financial stability, (4) ministry approval (MoD or MSI). Not blanket - justify each position with specific defense production duties. Technical roles qualify (engineers, DevOps, architects, PMs), generic roles rarely qualify (admin, sales, marketing). Quarterly review, can revoke if output drops.

4. Apply for critical-enterprise status before needing it (2-4 month process)

Waiting until engineers receive mobilization notices = too late. Best practice: file during scale-up (10-15 employees) when output proves criticality but before team large enough that mobilization becomes statistically probable. Extensive documentation required proving defense contribution and role criticality.

5. MoD hiring civilian PMs/DevOps with reservation creates talent competition

MoD offers: Senior/Middle PMs, POs, Middle-Senior BAs, DevOps, SysAdmins as civilian posts with reservation. Government reservation more secure (not tied to quarterly reviews). But bureaucratic constraints limit impact velocity. Startup counter: emphasize code-to-combat speed (weeks vs govt bureaucracy) for mission-driven engineers who value impact.
